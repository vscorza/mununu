### Why §A2 didn't deliver

The plan §A2 reasoning held one assumption: that the modal-evaluation hot path *already had a LabelSetId in hand*. In practice, the four `contains_key` consumer sites in `mu_calculus/evaluator.rs` start with a transition's labels, build a fresh `SmallVec` of just-the-uncontrollable-labels, and probe.

With SmallVec keys (original): one HashMap probe with SmallVec hash.

With LabelSetId keys (this EXP): one `find_label_set` probe (still SmallVec-hash on the table index) PLUS one HashMap probe with u32 hash. **Two probes where there was one.** The u32 saving is dwarfed by the duplicated SmallVec work; for transitions whose uncontrollable subset isn't in the table, the second probe is wasted.

For composition (the largest regressions), the build path now interns every transition's label set; composition doesn't read the table back, so it's pure overhead.

### The drop-in pattern at three EXPs

EXP-0010 (FxHashMap drop-in for composition): falsified.
EXP-0003 (LabelSetTable drop-in for SmallVec keys): falsified.
EXP-0002 (originally claimed 5-7×, contaminated by cache state): retracted.

Three "obvious wins" that weren't. Common failure mode: the hot path isn't where the optimization targeted. RefCell+Rc churn, SmallVec construction, and fixture iteration dominate.

The wins so far come from **structural changes to data layout / growth strategy**, not from **changing the hasher or key shape**:
- EXP-0002b SoA: changed iteration_ranks layout from HashMap to Vec<Vec>.
- EXP-0004: dropped a custom growth wrapper for Vec's native doubling.

Both restructured the access pattern. Neither was a 1-line drop-in.

This is the headline finding for blog post 2: **"Three drop-in hypotheses, three regressions; two structural changes, two wins. The protocol distinguished them at L3."**
