### Why FxHashMap loses on this workload

Three converging reasons documented in EXP-0010 README:
1. The hot map keys aren't simple integers — they include `Vec<String>` (label intern) and 4-tuple structural keys.
2. The maps are small (<2k entries) — at these sizes, FxHash's clustering hurts more than its faster hash helps.
3. Hot path is dominated by RefCell::borrow_mut + Rc::clone + Vec allocation — the hash itself is a small fraction.

This is exactly the kind of "obvious drop-in" the L3 protocol is designed to falsify before it lands as a public claim. ADR-0009 generalizes the lesson.

### What this sitting reveals about the protocol

- **L3 catches falsifications**, not just confirmations. EXP-0010 is the first plan-§-falsification finding in this programme. It's worth calling out in the blog series — falsification stories are rarer than success stories in perf literature.
- **The protocol's gates have bugs too.** Two bugs in `bench_diff.sh` got found and fixed in one sitting. The lesson: dogfood the gate on every EXP, not just on positive results.
- **Plan hypotheses need empirical re-evaluation.** §B2 was written on intuition (FxHash on integer pairs); empirical reality differs. Each remaining plan item should be re-rated based on similar workload-specific reasoning.
