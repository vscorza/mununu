\begin{cheatbox}{Formula Inversion}
Duality table --- swap operators to invert a formula:

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l c l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Controller} & $\longleftrightarrow$ & \textbf{Environment} \\
\midrule
\code{mu} & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{nu} \\
\code{nu} & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{mu} \\
\code{[]} (box) & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{<>} (diamond) \\
\code{<>} (diamond) & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{[]} (box) \\
\code{\&\&} & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{||} \\
\code{||} & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{\&\&} \\
\code{ctrl = controllable} & $\leftrightarrow$ & \code{ctrl = environment} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\smallskip
Inverting a formula swaps the roles of controller and environment: the environment becomes the ``player'' trying to satisfy the inverted property.
\end{cheatbox}

\begin{cheatbox}{Syntax}
A counterstrategy formula targets the \emph{complement} of the controller's goal:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=ctxdsl]
body = nu X. ((!target) &&
    < (ctrl = environment) > X);
\end{lstlisting}

This computes the set of states from which the environment can \textbf{avoid} \code{target} forever.
\end{cheatbox}

\columnbreak

%% ---- COLUMN 2 ----

\begin{cheatbox}{Environment Diamond}
\code{< (ctrl = environment) > phi} means:
\begin{itemize}
  \item There \textbf{exists} an uncontrollable transition leading to $\varphi$, OR
  \item \textbf{All} controllable transitions lead to $\varphi$.
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
The environment ``wins'' a step if it can force $\varphi$ via an uncontrollable move, or if every controllable option the controller might pick also leads to $\varphi$.
\end{cheatbox}

\begin{tipbox}{Skolem Paradigm}
Nondeterminism is adversarial for \textbf{both} players.  The controller chooses a label, not a transition; similarly, the environment chooses a label.  Multiple targets under the same label are resolved adversarially against the choosing player.
\end{tipbox}

\begin{cheatbox}{Common Patterns}
\textbf{Env prevents target:}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=ctxdsl]
nu X. ((!target) &&
    < (ctrl = environment) > X)
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Env counterstrategy (liveness):}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=ctxdsl]
mu X. (bad || < (ctrl = environment) > X)
\end{lstlisting}
Environment can force the system into \code{bad}.
\end{cheatbox}

\begin{cheatbox}{CLI}
Generate a counterstrategy graph:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=shell]
mununu context graph file.ctxdsl \
    --counterstrategy \
    --formula F \
    --automaton A \
    --output cs.html
\end{lstlisting}
The output highlights the environment's winning region and the transitions it uses to defeat the controller.
\end{cheatbox}
