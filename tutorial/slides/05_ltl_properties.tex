\begin{frame}{LTL Operators}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{6.5cm}@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Syntax} & \textbf{Operator} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
  \midrule
  \texttt{G}  & $\Globally$  (Globally)  & $\varphi$ holds in \emph{every} future state \\
  \texttt{F}  & $\Finally$   (Finally)   & $\varphi$ holds in \emph{some} future state \\
  \texttt{X}  & $\Next$      (Next)      & $\varphi$ holds in the \emph{next} state \\
  \texttt{U}  & $\Until$     (Until)     & $\psi$ holds eventually; $\varphi$ holds until then \\
  \texttt{W}  & Weak Until  & Like $\Until$, but $\psi$ need not occur \\
  \texttt{R}  & Release     & Dual of $\Until$: $\psi$ holds until $\varphi \wedge \psi$ \\
  \texttt{->} & Implies     & Standard logical implication \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5em}
In Mununu, LTL formulas are written with \ctxdsl{body = ltl ...;} and
automatically translated to mu-calculus for evaluation.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Three Property Types}

\lstinputlisting[style=ctxdsl, firstline=51, lastline=74]{../examples/05_ltl_properties.ctxdsl}

\begin{description}
  \item[Safety] $\Globally\varphi$ --- ``nothing bad ever happens.''
  \item[Liveness] $\Globally(\varphi \to \Finally\psi)$ --- ``every request gets a response.''
  \item[Response] $\Globally(\text{Full} \to \Finally\text{Empty})$ --- ``the system cycles.''
\end{description}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Game-Theoretic Reachability}

Can the controller \emph{force} the system to reach \textbf{Full},
despite the environment choosing \ctxdsl{idle}?

\lstinputlisting[style=ctxdsl, firstline=98, lastline=113]{../examples/05_ltl_properties.ctxdsl}

\medskip
\begin{itemize}
  \item \ctxdsl{[ (ctrl = controllable) ] X} = \textbf{controllable attractor step}:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item ALL uncontrollable transitions must stay in $X$.
      \item At least ONE controllable transition must go to $X$.
    \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Result:} 2/4 states satisfy (Filling, Full) ---
        \ctxdsl{idle} is uncontrollable, so the environment can loop at Empty forever.
  \item If \ctxdsl{idle} were controllable, the result would be 4/4.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Syntax: LTL vs.\ Mu-Calculus}

Both formula styles use the same \ctxdsl{formula} block:

\begin{columns}[t]
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{LTL syntax:}
\begin{ctxdslcode}
formula ltl_safety {
    over Tank;
    body = ltl G (Empty || Filling
                || Full || Draining);
}
\end{ctxdslcode}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Mu-calculus syntax:}
\begin{ctxdslcode}
formula mu_safety {
    over Tank;
    body = nu X. ([] X);
}
\end{ctxdslcode}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{1em}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Use \ctxdsl{body = ltl ...;} for temporal logic patterns.
  \item Use \ctxdsl{body = ...;} (no \texttt{ltl} keyword) for mu-calculus.
  \item Mununu translates LTL to mu-calculus internally before evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\end{frame}
