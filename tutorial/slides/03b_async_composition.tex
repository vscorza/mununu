\begin{frame}{System Overview: Two Sensors + Monitor}

Three independent components communicating via shared labels:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SensorA} --- cycles between \ctxdsl{IdleA} and \ctxdsl{SampledA}
  \item \textbf{SensorB} --- cycles between \ctxdsl{IdleB} and \ctxdsl{SampledB}
  \item \textbf{Monitor} --- receives reports: \ctxdsl{Ready}, \ctxdsl{ReceivedA}, \ctxdsl{ReceivedB}
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{Key difference from synchronous:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Any component can step \emph{independently} --- no lock-step.
  \item No \ctxdsl{skip} labels needed.
  \item Shared labels (e.g.\ \ctxdsl{sensor_a_report}) still synchronize the components that use them.
  \item Non-shared labels execute as \emph{interleaved} steps.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.5em}
\textbf{State space:} $2 \times 2 \times 3 = 12$ composed states.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Synchronous vs.\ Asynchronous Comparison}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Aspect} & \textbf{Synchronous} & \textbf{Asynchronous} \\
  \midrule
  Execution model  & Lock-step (all move together) & Interleaved (any can move) \\
  Skip labels      & Required for ``no-op'' steps  & Not needed \\
  Shared labels    & All participate on every step  & Only involved parties sync \\
  State space      & Product of states              & Product of states \\
  Typical use      & Tightly coupled components     & Independent components \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{1em}
\textbf{Rule of thumb:} use \alert{synchronous} when components must coordinate on every step;
use \alert{asynchronous} when components act independently and synchronize only on shared events.

\end{frame}
